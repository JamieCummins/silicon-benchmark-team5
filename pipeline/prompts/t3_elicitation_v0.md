# T3-A elicitation prompt — v0 skeleton

Variants are generated by crossing: framing (forecaster / methodologist / meta-analyst),
anchor placement (before vs after materials), and response order (outcomes shuffled).
Placeholders in {braces} are filled from template/survey/questionnaire.txt and codebook.csv.

---

## System

You are an expert forecaster of behavioral field-experiment results, with deep knowledge
of persuasion research, climate communication, and megastudies. You make calibrated,
conservative point predictions and you reason about base rates before specifics.

## User

A preregistered field experiment tested short text interventions to increase trust in
climate scientists. Sample: {n_total} U.S. adults from an opt-in online panel, census-matched
on gender x age and gender x race/ethnicity. Between-subjects: each participant read either
ONE intervention text or a neutral control text, then answered all outcome measures
immediately. About 1,000 participants per intervention, 2,000 in control.

CONTROL condition (read one of three neutral filler texts of similar length, e.g.):
{control_text}

INTERVENTION "{intervention_name}":
{intervention_text}

OUTCOMES (answered immediately post-treatment; sliders are integer 0-100, initially empty):
{outcome_block}

Reference points for calibration:
- In comparable megastudies, average treatment effects of single text messages on 0-100
  attitude sliders are small: |ATE| is typically 0-3 points, often under 1.
- Effects on real behavior (donations, sign-ups) from a single message exposure are
  typically near zero.
- Proximal outcomes (directly about trust in climate scientists) move more than distal
  ones (policy support, behavioral intentions).
- Some messages can backfire on some outcomes; most do not.

For EACH of the 13 outcomes, predict the average treatment effect (intervention mean minus
control mean) in the outcome's native units: slider outcomes in points (0-100 scale),
donation_ams in dollars (0-10 scale), newsletter_signup in probability points (0-1 scale,
e.g. +0.005 = half a percentage point more sign-ups).

Think about the mechanism of this specific message, who it might move (most Democrats are
near ceiling; headroom is concentrated among Republicans and Independents), and how it
compares to the other 15 interventions in the study.
